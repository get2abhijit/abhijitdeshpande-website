A career built on curves

My master’s degree was in computational geometry. Sixteen years later, geometry is still at the centre of what I do.

Where it started

I completed a master’s degree in mechanical engineering at the University of Wisconsin–Madison in 2010, with a focus on computational geometry: how to represent curves and surfaces in a computer, and how to compute with them reliably.

Why it stuck

Geometry has a quality I have always appreciated: its answers can be checked. A curve is smooth or it isn’t; two shapes intersect or they don’t. That makes it a demanding field — small numerical errors become visible very quickly — but also an honest one.

What it looks like today

Today I write CAD software for a living. The details of that work belong to my employer, but the themes are general: precision, robustness, and code that has to keep working for many years. Those themes find their way into my side projects too, even when the side project is a music website or a cricket app.

The other side of software

In my essay about building nihiramusic.in (opens in a new tab), I admitted that I can tell you whether a curve is perfectly smooth, but not whether a colour feels warm. That is still true. Building websites has turned out to be a useful way to practise the side of software where the answer can’t be checked with a formula.
