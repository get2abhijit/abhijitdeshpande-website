From the crease to the analyst’s laptop

Cricket has been part of my life as a player, a coach, a club organiser and a performance analyst. Each role has shown me the same game from a different angle.

Playing

Like most people in cricket, I started as a player. Batting teaches a particular kind of patience: one mistake can end your day, so you learn to judge every ball on its merits rather than on the last one.

Coaching

Becoming an ICC-certified Level 1 coach changed the way I watch the game. A coach has to put into words what a player simply feels, and do it in a way that helps someone else improve. That is harder than it sounds, and it made me a better observer.

Building a club

Setting up a local club in Germany and running it as it climbed to Bundesliga level was a different kind of work altogether: players, practice, fixtures, equipment and all the administration that keeps a team on the field. Serving on the board and committees of my regional association showed me how much effort goes into growing a sport that is still small in this country.

Analysing

As a performance analyst, I have worked with the German and the Luxembourg national teams. The job is to turn video and numbers into a few clear observations that a player or a coach can act on. More data is rarely the problem; choosing what matters is.

Where software comes in

That last point is also where CricketIQ fits. Analysis is where my two worlds meet: the engineer in me wants the data to be clean and reproducible, and the cricketer in me wants the answer to be useful on match day.
